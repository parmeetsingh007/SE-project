RBI payment/authentication guideline excerpts (paraphrased summaries for course
use, not verbatim circular text)

### RBI | AFA-1 | Additional Factor Authentication for Digital Payments
Card-not-present and other digital payment transactions above a specified value
threshold must be authenticated using an Additional Factor of Authentication
(AFA) beyond the static password or PIN, such as an OTP, biometric, or a
dedicated authentication app.

### RBI | AFA-2 | Risk-Based Step-Up Authentication
Beyond the standard AFA value threshold, banks are expected to run risk-based
transaction monitoring and trigger step-up authentication automatically for any
transaction flagged as high risk by their fraud detection systems, regardless of
transaction value.

### RBI | SMS-OTP-1 | One-Time Password Validity and Reuse
One-time passwords issued for authenticating a payment must have a limited
validity window and must not be reusable after a successful or failed
verification attempt or after expiry.

### RBI | LOCKOUT-1 | Failed Authentication Attempt Handling
After a defined number of consecutive failed authentication attempts on a
payment channel or instrument, that channel/instrument must be temporarily
blocked, the customer notified, and any subsequent unblocking must follow a
defined, auditable process rather than being automatic and unannounced.

### RBI | TURNAROUND-1 | Authentication Friction and Abandonment
Authentication steps should not introduce undue friction that causes customers
to abandon otherwise legitimate transactions. Guidance recommends completing
authentication promptly, though it does not prescribe a single hard numeric
response-time SLA that applies to every channel.

### RBI | GRIEVANCE-1 | Customer Grievance Redress and Liability
Banks must provide customers with a grievance redress mechanism for disputed or
failed digital payment authentication events, including limited/zero customer
liability protections in specified unauthorized-transaction scenarios where the
customer promptly reports the issue.
