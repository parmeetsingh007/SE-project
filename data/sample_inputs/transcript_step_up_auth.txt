Interview transcript
Topic: Step-up authentication for high-risk payments
Participants: Priya Nair (Business Analyst), Marcus Webb (Security Lead)
Date: 2026-02-11

Priya: Thanks for making time, Marcus. So the ask from the product side is basically
"add step-up authentication for payments above a risk threshold." Can you walk me
through what that means from your side?

Marcus: Sure. Right now everything above our fraud model's risk score just goes
through with a one-time password over SMS, same as every other transaction. What
we want is: if a payment gets flagged as high risk, we stop and ask the customer
for something stronger before we let it through.

Priya: When you say "something stronger," what specifically?

Marcus: Honestly, it depends. For app users we'd want biometric — fingerprint or
face, whatever the phone supports. For web we'd probably fall back to an
authenticator app code. I haven't fully worked out the web case though, that's
still being discussed with the mobile team.

Priya: Okay, let's flag that as open. What's the risk threshold — is that a fixed
number or does it move?

Marcus: It's a score from the fraud engine, 0 to 100. Anything over 70 today
triggers extra review by an analyst manually. For step-up auth we want it to kick
in automatically, no analyst in the loop, but honestly I'm not 100% sure 70 is the
right cutoff for this specific feature — might need to be higher so we're not
annoying legitimate customers. We'd want to tune it after launch probably.

Priya: Got it, so the threshold itself might be configurable. What happens if the
customer fails the step-up challenge?

Marcus: They get three attempts, then the transaction is declined and we log it
as a potential fraud event. After that they'd need to call support to unblock the
account, actually wait — I think it's supposed to auto-unlock after 24 hours too,
but don't quote me on that, I'd need to check with the fraud ops team on the exact
lockout policy.

Priya: No problem, I'll mark that as needs follow-up. From a compliance angle, is
there anything specific driving this, or is it just general good practice?

Marcus: It's partly RBI's guidelines on additional factor authentication for
digital payments above certain value bands, and partly PCI-DSS expectations
around strong customer authentication for card-not-present transactions. Compliance
gave us a memo on this a few weeks back — I can dig it up if it's useful for you.

Priya: That would help a lot, please send it over. Last thing — any requirement
around how fast this needs to respond? Customers waiting ten seconds for a
fingerprint prompt is going to feel broken.

Marcus: Yeah, agreed, it should feel instant. I don't have a hard number handed
down from anywhere, but personally I'd say the challenge should be presented to
the user in under two seconds after the risky transaction is detected. That's just
my gut feel though, not an official SLA.

Priya: That's useful context, I'll write it up as a draft with a note that the
number needs sign-off. Thanks, Marcus.
